Nearly everything written about AI for small businesses is about getting better output
from a chat window. Better prompts, better tools, better wording.

All of it shares one assumption: that you are sitting there.

For someone running a small business, that assumption is the entire problem. The
bottleneck was never the quality of the answer. It is that nothing happens on a day you
are too busy to open the app, and the days you are too busy are the days you most need
something watching.

## The shape of the alternative

Instead of one assistant you talk to, a few narrow ones with written jobs, reading files
you maintain, running on a schedule, producing something you read rather than something
you prompt.

I call it a board. The name matters less than the four properties:

- **Narrow.** Each seat owns one thing. A seat that owns everything produces general
  observations, which is another way of saying nothing.
- **Written down.** Its job lives in a file, not in your head or in a chat history.
- **Scheduled.** It runs on Tuesday whether or not you thought about it on Tuesday.
- **Reading something real.** It reads your actual numbers, not your recollection of them.

None of that requires code. It requires files and a schedule, and the files are plain
text you can read and edit.

## What it actually produces

Not answers to questions. A **meeting**: a short standing report that says what changed,
what is below the line, what it does not know, and what it thinks you should decide.

The difference from a chat is that it arrives whether you asked or not, and it is about
the same things every week, so you can see movement instead of snapshots.

## The five things that make it work

Each of these fails in a specific way if you skip it, and I have written about most of
them separately:

1. **A charter, not a prompt** — [why your AI forgets your business every
   time](/blog/prompt-vs-charter-ai-forgets-your-business), and the one line in a charter
   that does most of the work.
2. **Floors, not targets** — [why it tells you everything looks
   fine](/blog/why-your-ai-says-everything-is-fine) no matter what kind of week it was.
3. **A run on a schedule** — and [the check that catches it when the schedule dies
   silently](/blog/scheduled-ai-tasks-fail-silently), which it will.
4. **A review habit** — [four checks that separate a useful report from a plausible
   one](/blog/how-to-check-ai-output-against-real-numbers).
5. **Autonomy earned in steps** — what it may do alone today, and what it has to earn the
   right to do. Written down, and revocable.

## What this does not do

It does not run your business. It produces analysis and drafts. The decisions and the
work stay yours, and any version of this that claims otherwise is selling something.

It also will not fix a business problem that is not an information problem. If you know
exactly what is wrong and have not had time to fix it, a board will tell you the same
thing on a schedule.

What it does fix is the specific failure of finding out too late, because nothing was
watching on the weeks you were heads-down.

## Start with one seat

You do not need the whole thing. One seat, one floor, four files, a weekly run. That is
an afternoon, and it will tell you more about your week than any chat window has.
