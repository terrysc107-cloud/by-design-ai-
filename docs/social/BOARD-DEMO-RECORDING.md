# The board demo — recording plan

One recording session. Produces the long-form walkthrough **and** roughly a
dozen clips. Beat sheet, not a script: bullets to freestyle from, with the
handful of lines that must be said word for word marked **MUST SAY**.

**No date is promoted anywhere in this recording.** The CTA is recorded as a
separate take at the very end precisely so it can be swapped later without
touching anything else.

---

## 1. Answering the setup question first

**Yes, copy the template live. That is the demo.** Watching an empty folder
become a working board is the whole product in one shot, and no amount of
talking about it lands the same way.

But do it as **two boards, not one**:

| | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Board A** | The template, copied fresh on camera, set up from nothing | This is you in ten minutes. Concrete, honest, repeatable. |
| **Board B** | A real board with real history | This is what it looks like after three months. Board A alone has no output to show yet, so it stays abstract. |

Only Board A gets built live. Board B you cut to.

### 🔴 Board B has real numbers in it

Your live boards read ATS enrolment, crcst revenue, real pipeline, real customer
names. **Screen-sharing one of those puts all of it on tape, permanently, in a
file you then publish.**

Before recording, make a **redacted copy** of whichever board you use as Board B.
Round the numbers, replace names with roles, keep the structure and the shape of
the output exactly as it is. The lesson is in the structure, not the values, and
nobody watching can tell the difference.

Recommendation: use **ATS** as Board B. It is genuinely established, the numbers
are real, and rounding them costs the demo nothing. Do not use aixdesign itself,
where the honest numbers today are one subscriber and no revenue.

### Rehearse Board A once, then record

Do a full dry run before you press record. A first run that hangs for ninety
seconds on camera is ninety dead seconds, and you will not be able to cut it
cleanly because you will be talking over it.

Then record it for real, and **keep the mistakes that teach something**. Cut only
the ones that are just waiting.

### Record in takes, not in one pass

This is the single thing that will save you the most work. Each segment below
starts clean and ends clean, as its own take. Stop between them. Re-do any one
of them without re-doing the rest.

If you record 60 minutes continuously, you will spend hours hunting for clip
boundaries and every clip will start mid-sentence. Recorded in takes, the clips
already exist when you stop recording, and the long-form is just the assembly.

## 2. Setup checklist

**Before you press record.**

- [ ] Redacted Board B prepared, opened, and checked for real names and numbers
- [ ] Board A: template repo ready to copy, **signed into the right GitHub account**
- [ ] Rehearsal run done, so you know how long the first run actually takes
- [ ] Desktop cleared: no other client work, no revenue dashboards, no email, no
      Slack previews, no browser tabs with other businesses in the titles
- [ ] Notifications off, system-wide, both machine and phone
- [ ] Terminal and editor font size up. What is readable to you at arm's length
      is unreadable on a phone
- [ ] Screen recording at 1080p minimum, and check the aspect. You need a 16:9
      master; verticals get cropped from it later
- [ ] Mic check, thirty seconds, played back. Do not discover the room echo in
      the edit
- [ ] Water within reach, out of frame
- [ ] This document open on a second screen, not the recorded one

## 3. The segments

Roughly 55 to 65 minutes of raw material.

### SEG 1 — Cold open, the problem · 2-3 min

The hook, straight in. No introduction, no "hey everyone", no housekeeping.

- Your AI says everything looks fine. Every week.
- That is not agreeableness. You gave it targets instead of floors, so nothing
  can be off track.
- The bigger problem: it only works on the days you sit down and open it.
  Nothing persists. You re-explain your business to a chat window every time.
- What this is instead: a board. A few narrow assistants that read your real
  numbers on a schedule and hand you a meeting.

**Clip:** the strongest 45 to 60 seconds, vertical. This is the top-of-funnel
hook and the one that has to stop a scroll.

### SEG 2 — What a board actually is · 3-4 min

Show the empty structure while you talk.

- Not a chatbot. Not an app. A folder of markdown files, a seat, and a schedule.
- You can read every file. There is nothing hidden and no vendor in the middle.
- Name the loop out loud: Brief, Gather, Run, Review, Decide, Act.
- **MUST SAY:** the board does not act. It produces analysis and drafts. The
  decisions and the work stay yours.

**Clip:** "What is an AI board", 60 seconds.

### SEG 3 — Copy the template · 4-5 min

- Go to `github.com/terrysc107-cloud/ai-board`
- **Use this template → Create a new repository → set it to Private**
- Name it `ai-board`, so every later reference matches what you are looking at

**🔴 MUST SAY, and slow down here:**

> "Use this template. Not Fork. A fork of a public repo is permanently public
> and you cannot make it private afterwards. This folder is about to fill up
> with your revenue and your customers' names."

This is the highest-stakes thirty seconds in the recording. If someone copies
the gesture wrong, they publish their business. Say it, show it, and say it
again as they click.

**Clip:** "The setting people get wrong", 60 to 90 seconds. Genuinely useful,
highly shareable, and it protects people. Likely your best-performing clip after
SEG 7.

### SEG 4 — The four files · 10-12 min

The richest segment. Fill each one in with real content as you go. Four separate
beats, so it cuts into four clips.

**`GOALS.md`** — floors, and what you ruled out
- **MUST SAY:** a floor, not a target. Give a board targets and it reports that
  everything looks fine, forever, because nothing can be off track.
- Convert one out loud, in front of them.

**`METRICS.md`** — numbers, each with its source
- Every number carries where it came from, right next to it.
- A number with no source rots silently, and a rotted number looks exactly like
  a live one.

**`DECISION-LOG.md`** — choices, and what would reverse them
- The second half is the part everyone skips and the part that makes it useful
  in three months.

**`PIPELINE.md`** — who is in play, honestly
- Honestly is doing the work in that sentence. A board fed an optimistic
  pipeline produces optimistic nonsense.

**Clips:** four, one per file, 45 to 75 seconds each.

### SEG 5 — The charter · 5-6 min

- A prompt is what you ask. A charter is who it is.
- Walk through: who this seat is, what it may do alone, what floors it is
  measured against.
- **The money beat:** name something the seat will refuse to do, even when it
  would rather not. Write that line live.

**Clip:** "The line that makes it work", 60 to 90 seconds.

### SEG 6 — The first run · 6-8 min

- Brief it: what the run is for, what to read, what not to do.
- Run it.
- Read the output together, out loud.

If the real runtime is long, say so and cut. Do not fill silence by narrating a
progress bar.

**Clip:** the run, tightened, 60 seconds.

### SEG 7 — The bad run · 5 min

**The most important segment in the recording. Do not skip it and do not
sanitise it.**

- Show a run that produces a confidently wrong answer. Set it up so this
  actually happens rather than describing it.
- **MUST SAY:** a plausible number looks exactly like a real one.
- Trace one claim back to its source, live, and let it fail to have one.
- **MUST SAY:** if a number you need is missing, say it is missing. Do not
  estimate it.

Showing your system being wrong on camera is the most credible thing in the
entire recording, and it is what nobody else in this category does.

**Clip:** 60 to 90 seconds. Expect this to be your best clip overall.

### SEG 8 — The schedule · 4 min

- It runs on a day you never sit down. That is the whole point.
- Then the failure: a job that stops does not error. It produces nothing, and
  nothing looks exactly like a quiet week.
- Tell the 40-day story. Yours, specific, with the number.

**Clip:** "Scheduled work dies without telling you", 60 seconds.

### SEG 9 — Board B, three months in · 8-10 min

Cut to the redacted board.

- Open a real meeting output. Read part of it.
- Show the archive: dated, one file per run.
- **Show a correction.** Somewhere the board was wrong and how it noticed. This
  is the single most persuasive artifact you have, because everyone else's demo
  only shows the system winning.
- Show `memory/CANDIDATES.md`: what it proposes versus what you promote.

**Clips:** two or three. "What three months of this looks like", and the
correction as its own clip.

### SEG 10 — Close · 2 min, recorded as a separate take

**Record this last, on its own, and re-record it whenever the offer changes.**
That is why it is a separate take.

- What they can do next: read The Board Method guide.
- **MUST SAY:** independent educational product by AI by Design, not affiliated
  with or endorsed by Anthropic.
- **No date. No cohort. No seat count.** Nothing is promoted in this version.

When the Lab date goes public, re-record only this take and re-cut the tail of
the long-form. Nothing else changes.

## 4. What comes out of it

| Asset | From | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Long-form walkthrough, 25-35 min cut | Segments 1-10 assembled | YouTube, and the `/guide` follow-up |
| Hook clip | SEG 1 | Every channel. The top of the funnel |
| "What is an AI board" | SEG 2 | Every channel |
| "The setting people get wrong" | SEG 3 | High share value. Protects people |
| Four file clips | SEG 4 | One per week, evergreen |
| "The line that makes it work" | SEG 5 | The charter is the differentiated idea |
| The first run | SEG 6 | Proof it is real |
| **The bad run** | SEG 7 | Your best asset. Lead with it |
| "Scheduled work dies" | SEG 8 | Pairs with the existing social card |
| Three months in + the correction | SEG 9 | Credibility, and the Kit's whole pitch |

Roughly twelve clips plus one long-form, from one afternoon.

### Feeding the engine

- Stills from SEG 4 and SEG 9 become card backgrounds. The Playwright card
  renderer in the course repo at `social/` already produces four sizes.
- The five existing Board Method cards line up with SEG 3 through SEG 8, so the
  clips and the cards can ship as pairs rather than competing.
- Nothing can post until the AI by Design channels exist in Postiz and
  `POSTIZ_LINKEDIN_ID` is set. Record anyway; the library is the asset and it
  does not expire.

### Claim rules apply to the voiceover

Everything spoken here goes through the same gate as written copy: no income
claims, no speed multipliers, no student counts, no invented dates or seat
counts, no implied Anthropic affiliation. If a clip caption needs checking,
`npm run check:claims` in the aixdesign repo tests the same rules.

---

## 5. Order of work

1. Prepare and redact Board B
2. Dry run of Board A end to end, timed
3. Clear the desktop, kill notifications, mic check
4. Record SEG 1 through SEG 9 as separate takes
5. Record SEG 10 last, alone
6. Pull the twelve clips first, while the takes are fresh
7. Assemble the long-form from the same takes
