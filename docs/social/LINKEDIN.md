## 3. Post templates (one per content pillar)

Brand rule (from `BRAND-KIT.md` §9): post-ratio 2·1·2·1 per week
(2 build-in-public, 1 teardown, 2 primitives, 1 proof). Every post ends
with one of two CTAs — soft (guide) or hard (call).

LinkedIn formatting rules:
- **Line breaks** are the design system. No emoji-heavy walls.
- **First 210 chars** are the hook (above "…see more").
- **No links in the body** — links kill reach. Put the link in the
  **first comment** and reference it ("link in comments").
- **3–5 hashtags max**, at the end.

### 3a. Bottleneck teardown

```
A 14-person agency was losing 6 hours a week to one thing:
forwarding leads from Facebook → spreadsheet → CRM → Slack.

Four tools. Zero AI. Just bad plumbing.

We replaced it with a 9-step automation:
→ Lead hits the form
→ Enriched against Apollo
→ Scored against a Claude prompt
→ Routed to the right rep in Slack
→ Logged to GHL with the right tag
→ Booked, if hot

Build time: 4 hours.
Time saved per week: 6 hours.
Cost: less than what they were paying their VA to do step 1.

The lesson: most "AI projects" are systems projects in disguise.
Diagnose first. AI second. Build last.

What's your "lead from form to CRM" look like? Drop it below.

Free guide for the rest of the playbook → link in comments.

#aiforbusiness #aiautomation #businesssystems #aiconsulting
```

### 3b. Build in public

```
Shipping today: a Claude-powered intake reviewer.

Client gets discovery-call applications. 60% are junk. They were burning

So we built this:

→ Form submit → webhook
→ Claude reads the answers

Took one afternoon. Replaced the inbox triage entirely.

Stack: Next.js route handler + Claude API + Slack webhook + Supabase log.

Most useful prompt move? Telling Claude *who* it's screening for — not
```

### 3c. Primitive ("steal this")

```
Steal this prompt. We use it on every consulting engagement:

  You are an operations diagnostician.
  I will paste a description of how a task is done today.
  Your job:
  1. Map the task as a flow (steps + handoffs).
  2. Identify the *one* bottleneck that, if removed, would unlock 80%
     of the gain.
  3. Recommend the leanest fix — manual SOP, no-code automation, or
     custom build. Bias toward the leanest option.
  4. Estimate hours saved per week if implemented.
  Be skeptical. Don't recommend AI unless it's clearly the right tool.

Paste it into Claude or GPT.
Feed it your messiest workflow.
Watch what falls out.

This is half of what we do in a discovery call. The other half is
deciding what to actually build.

Want the full diagnostic kit? Link in comments.

#aiconsulting #systemsdesign #aiforbusiness #promptengineering
```

### 3d. Proof & principle

```
A principle from this month's build:

"The cheapest automation is the one you didn't have to build."

Client wanted us to automate their proposal-writing. We mapped it. Turns
out 80% of proposals were going to leads who never replied to the first
follow-up.

We didn't automate proposals.
We automated *the second follow-up*.

Reply rate jumped 41%. The proposal problem became smaller because the
funnel got tighter upstream.

Rule of thumb we now use:
→ Don't automate the symptom.
→ Move one step earlier in the chain.
→ Re-measure.

Most "we need AI" requests are actually "we need to fix step 2."

Book a free 30-min diagnostic → link in comments.

#aiconsulting #leanbusiness #operatingsystem #businesssystems
```

## 8. What to NOT do on LinkedIn (brand-protection)

- No "Excited to announce…" posts. Show, don't announce.
- No engagement-bait ("agree? 👇") with no substance above it.
- No reposting AI news without an original take.
- No tagging people who didn't agree to be tagged.
- No "broetry" — the 1-line-per-paragraph style is a tell. Use line
  breaks for *meaning*, not theatre.
- No "DM me 'GUIDE' for the free thing." Put the link in the comments
  like a human.
- No naming clients without written approval. Blur names; share numbers.
